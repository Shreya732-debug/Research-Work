Time- and Version-Aware Financial Claim Verification

    The project in 45 seconds
    Companies sometimes correct numbers in their financial reports. A financial AI assistant must therefore
    check more than whether a number appears in a document: it must use the right report version and the
    information available at the requested date. We are building a verifier that returns supported, contradicted
    or underdetermined, with cited evidence. We have a working rule-based prototype and 30 draft pilot cases.
    Human review is underway; evaluating and integrating existing language models is the next planned stage.

2. The specific research gap we are investigating
Established prior work: FinGround says it defaults to the most recent filing when restated figures conflict.[2] Fin-
VerBench describes discarding companies with source inconsistencies due to tagging variations or restatements.[3]
Our proposed focus: construct and review paired cases that test whether a verifier follows the requested
original/corrected version and historical cutoff, including a withdrawal before a replacement becomes available.
Check the exact metric, period, units and evidence along with the answer.
Honest novelty boundary: this is a literature-motivated candidate gap, not proof of a first-ever method. Temporal checking,
financial verification and uncertainty already have prior work. Closest released benchmarks still need fuller inspection.

3. What we will engineer, and where the AI fits
An LLM (large language model) is a model that reads and generates language. We plan to use existing LLMs
to interpret claims and extract evidence from reports. Explicit software checks then enforce the requested dates,
versions and numerical consistency. This is the proposed combined system; the present demo starts from prepared
facts.

Proposed contribution: a reviewed evaluation set and an evidence-linked verifier showing when disclosure version
and historical availability matter. The current simple withdrawal-aware rule matches the reference resolver on the
draft cases; that limits any algorithmic-superiority claim.[4]
What we need from the supervisor: agreement on the scope and expected AI implementation depth, feedback on
the evaluation design and a review of unresolved cases. Two calibrated students can check explicit filing evidence;
difficult accounting interpretations remain unresolved or are excluded. We will not claim expert validation.
